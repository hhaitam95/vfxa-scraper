TITLE: Debris
OVERVIEW: This lesson introduces debris as an effects-adjacent category that often overlaps with props, especially when objects break, shatter, transform, or become airborne. It focuses on drawing small pieces rotating in perspective and on animating materials such as paper or drapery that produce complex overlapping motion. It is aimed at effects artists who may need to take over prop-like breakup and secondary-motion shots.
KEY TOPICS:
- Debris versus props
- Breaking and shattering
- Rotation in perspective
- Rocks, glass, ice, leaves, and foliage
- Paper and drapery
- Overlap and twisting
- Heavy versus light debris
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- End-over-end rotation drawing
- Perspective construction
- Frame-by-frame debris animation
- Overlap and sliding motion
- Weight-driven timing
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Determine whether a prop has become an effect because it is breaking, shattering, transforming, or otherwise creating effect-like motion.
2. Choose representative debris pieces and establish their three-dimensional rotation rather than simply translating a flat shape.
3. Animate rocks, glass, ice, leaves, or foliage with perspective changes that reveal different sides of the object.
4. Add secondary overlap for flexible objects such as paper or a cape, allowing wind and forces to create folds, twists, and delayed motion.
5. For shattering objects, vary piece size, rotation, overlap, and sliding behavior according to the object's weight.

- Observe real falling paper and drapery to study overlapping motion.
- Vary debris according to size, weight, and breakup behavior rather than making every piece identical.
- Be prepared for effects work to include prop-like animation when objects break apart.
