TITLE: Arcs
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains why arcs are a fundamental motion pattern in animation and why natural movement rarely travels as a perfectly straight line. It connects arcs to gravity, body mechanics, and two-dimensional motion, then applies the idea to effects such as splashes. It is aimed at artists who need to make motion feel more natural and visually interesting.
KEY TOPICS:
- Arcs in two-dimensional motion
- Gravity and naturalistic motion
- X/Y movement and combined trajectories
- Timing and easing along an arc
- Arcs in splashes and airborne elements
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Timing charts for frame spacing
- Tracking an object's path through successive positions
- Using easing to place more frames near the top of an arc
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Identify whether the object is moving in more than one dimension; when x and y movement combine, think in terms of an arc.
2. Plot the main positions and avoid connecting them with a simple straight line when the action is affected by gravity or changing direction.
3. Apply easing so the object slows near the top of a gravity-driven arc and accelerates again as it falls.
4. Track every secondary piece of the effect along its own arc rather than letting it abruptly stop or dissipate.
5. Follow each arc all the way through its natural motion before ending the effect.

- When an effect dissipates, do not let dissipation override the established physical path.
- Give every airborne fragment enough follow-through to respect its energy.
