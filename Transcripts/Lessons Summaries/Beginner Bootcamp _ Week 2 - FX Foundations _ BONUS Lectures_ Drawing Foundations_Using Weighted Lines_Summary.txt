TITLE: Using Weighted Lines
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates how varying line thickness can separate planes, suggest light and shadow, direct the viewer's eye, and make sketches feel more expressive. It emphasizes choosing a consistent line-weight system and then applying it repeatedly across the drawing. It is intended for artists developing clearer and more dimensional FX sketches.
KEY TOPICS:
- Line-weight systems
- Plane separation
- Light and shadow
- Center of attention
- Natural line flow
- Softening edges
- Suggesting forms with partial edges
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Thick outer contour
- Medium internal edges
- Thin texture/detail lines
- Pressure variation for light/shadow
- Corner-only line indications
- Softened corners
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Decide the line-weight hierarchy before drawing and keep the rule consistent.
2. Use the thickest lines for outer edges, medium lines for structural/internal edges, and thinner lines for small details.
3. Vary thickness according to lighting, making darker or less-lit edges heavier than lit edges.
4. Increase or decrease thickness around a chosen focal point so the line flow guides the viewer.
5. Follow the natural stroke direction by varying pressure as the line travels.
6. Soften corners and use only partial edge marks when a full outline would be too heavy, allowing the viewer's brain to complete the form.

- Keep the same logic across the entire sketch.
- Try combining line-weight hierarchy with focal-point emphasis.
- Partial contours can be useful when you want the viewer to infer the form rather than drawing every edge.
