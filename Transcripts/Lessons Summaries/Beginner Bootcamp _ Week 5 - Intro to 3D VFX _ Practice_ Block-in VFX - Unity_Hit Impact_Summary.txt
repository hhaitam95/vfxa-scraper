TITLE: Unity Hit Impact
OVERVIEW: This lesson builds a fast hit impact from a flare, triangular spikes, color changes, velocity, and stretched billboards. It emphasizes how tiny timing changes, contrast, direction, and controlled randomness can give a very short effect a strong story arc and physicality. It is aimed at beginners learning to make punchy gameplay feedback in Unity.
KEY TOPICS:
- Fast impact timing
- Contrast and color progression
- Flare and spike layers
- Radial motion
- Stretched billboards
- Velocity alignment
- Velocity damping
- Small-range randomness
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Universal Render Pipeline > Particles > Unlit
- Transparent surface type
- Burst emission
- Random lifetime/size
- Sphere spawn shape
- Velocity over Lifetime > Radial
- Renderer > Stretched Billboard
- Texture orientation toward positive X for stretched billboards
- Limit Velocity over Lifetime
- Dampen
- Speed curve
- Color over Lifetime
- Second Inspector tab with lock
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Create and duplicate simple particle materials for the flare, triangle, and ring textures.
2. Build the flare as a single short-lived burst, keeping it stationary and using a quick size-over-lifetime curve with a slight end hold.
3. Duplicate the setup for spike particles, use a small sphere spawn region, add randomized radial velocity and lifetime/size ranges, and switch the renderer to Stretched Billboard.
4. Make sure the source texture is oriented correctly so the stretched billboard follows particle velocity visually.
5. Add a small gravity contribution for physicality, then use Limit Velocity over Lifetime with damping and a speed curve to control how quickly spikes lose motion.
6. Use Color over Lifetime to make the first moment white-hot, then cool the particles toward blue/aqua over their short lives.
7. Compare against a reference effect using a locked second Inspector and make final changes only after judging the complete hit.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Small timing differences matter greatly in very short effects.
- Keep random ranges narrow when you want organic variation without chaos.
- Use Stretched Billboard when the particle's direction should be visually obvious.
- Disable looping before treating the result as a real gameplay hit.
- Keep the hottest value at the impact moment and let later colors cool quickly.
