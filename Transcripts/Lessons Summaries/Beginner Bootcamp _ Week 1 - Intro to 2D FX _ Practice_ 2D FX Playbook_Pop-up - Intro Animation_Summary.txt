TITLE: Pop-up
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates an appearance where an effect arrives already large, briefly settles through a larger key stage, and then lands on its final size. It first explores a triangular shape with varied corner timing and then turns the same planning into a liquid splat. It is aimed at artists who need readable, punchy appearances that do not begin from a tiny point.
KEY TOPICS:
- Pop-up appearance
- Key stages and minimum readable timing
- Corner-by-corner variation
- Motion guides
- Splat construction
- Liquid stretching and settling
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Keyframe planning
- Guide lines from the screen center
- Onion Skin
- Oval brush
- Canvas rotation for drawing symmetry
- Frame extension
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Define the initial large state, an intermediate/key stage, and the final smaller state.
2. Ensure there is at least one frame showing entry into each key stage, then remove extra frames when tighter timing is needed.
3. Add variation by letting different corners travel different distances and begin returning at different times.
4. Use the point-motion guides to keep the shape organized while exaggerating the movement.
5. Reuse the same corner movement to build a splat: animate the central blob, add differently sized bubbles, then draw the stretched connecting material.
6. Use onion skin and canvas rotation to keep the organic connections readable while settling the final form.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Optimize the number of frames when the timing needs to be tight, but keep enough information for the appearance to read.
- Avoid having every point of a shape move identically.
- Use motion guides to track pointy or complex forms.
- Reuse a planned motion as a base for another effect instead of starting from nothing.
