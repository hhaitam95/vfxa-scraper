TITLE: Timing Offset - Looping Effects
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates a loop made from multiple appearances that start and finish at different points in the cycle. One smoke blob spans the loop boundary while a second begins later, and smaller pieces are staggered through the same cycle. It is intended for artists learning how timing offsets can hide the start/end seam of a looping effect.
KEY TOPICS:
- Loop boundaries
- Offset timing
- Overlapping appearances and disappearances
- Two-repeat loop checking
- Secondary staggered pieces
- Gradual emergence and dissipation
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- After Fill
- Ctrl to connect the start/end of a stroke
- Mark Frame
- Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V to duplicate loop frames
- Onion Skin
- Layer opacity for easier drawing
- Multiple offset motions
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Mark the loop start and end and work with two repeats of the timeline so the transition can be judged across the seam.
2. Draw the first blob so it grows, travels, slows, and becomes small by the loop end, then continue its final disappearance into the beginning of the next repeat.
3. Add a second blob whose lifecycle begins around the middle of the first motion, continuing across the loop boundary at a different offset.
4. Connect the larger shapes enough that the seam does not reveal a hard transition; add further offsets rather than forcing two shapes to meet unnaturally.
5. Add smaller upward-dissipating bits at staggered frames and offset both their starts and their endings.
6. Review the full cycle and vary shape, height, timing, and size when repeated pieces begin to look too similar.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Always inspect a loop across the seam rather than only watching one repeat.
- Use several offsets for more complex loops.
- Keep adjacent appearances visually distinct through height, scale, shape, and timing.
- For After Fill, ensure the stroke closes; Ctrl can help connect the endpoints while drawing.
