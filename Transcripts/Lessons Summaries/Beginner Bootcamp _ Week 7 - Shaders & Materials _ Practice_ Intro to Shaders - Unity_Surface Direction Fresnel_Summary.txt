TITLE: Surface Direction Fresnel
OVERVIEW: This lesson uses surface direction and the dot product to build a Fresnel-style edge mask that reacts to how a surface faces the camera. It demonstrates how normalized vectors and surface normals can produce a view-dependent transition and then uses the result for glow-like color/opacity behavior. It is intended for artists learning a practical vector-based method for world-interacting materials.

STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a surface and compare its normal direction with the direction toward the camera.
2. Use Dot Product to turn their directional relationship into a scalar value.
3. Normalize the vectors so length does not distort the directional comparison.
4. Remap or power the scalar so the strongest values appear around the desired viewing angle.
5. Connect the resulting mask to color or opacity to create a Fresnel/rim-like material response.
6. Test the setup on different meshes, including curved or spiral shapes, and observe how the edge response changes with view direction.
