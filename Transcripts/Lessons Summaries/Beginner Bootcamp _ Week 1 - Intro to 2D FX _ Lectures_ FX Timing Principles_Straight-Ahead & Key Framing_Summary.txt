TITLE: Straight-Ahead & Key Framing
OVERVIEW: This lesson compares straight-ahead animation, where the artist discovers the motion sequentially, with keyframe animation, where important poses are planned before the in-betweens. It explains the creative and production trade-offs of each approach and shows how keyframes and breakdowns reduce uncertainty in complex motion. It is aimed at artists deciding how to structure an effect or animation efficiently.
KEY TOPICS:
- Straight-ahead animation
- Keyframe animation
- Breakdowns and in-betweens
- Planning versus improvisation
- Production deadlines
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Keyframe, breakdown, and in-between planning
- Straight-ahead frame-by-frame drawing
- Timing and pose planning
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Decide whether the action benefits from discovery and spontaneity or from deliberate planning.
2. For straight-ahead work, carry the intended motion in your head and draw sequentially while discovering the result.
3. For keyframing, establish the first and final poses, then add a mid-frame/breakdown that describes the main action.
4. Add in-between drawings after the anchors are clear, using as many keyframes and breakdowns as the animation requires.
5. Choose the approach based on the situation, familiarity with the effect, desired quality, available time, and creative goals.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Try animating the same effect with both methods to learn which is more efficient for that kind of motion.
- Straight-ahead can sacrifice some planning; keyframing normally requires more preparation but reduces guesswork.
- Use multiple keyframes and breakdowns when the action is too complex for a single start/mid/end setup.
