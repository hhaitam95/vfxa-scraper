TITLE: Ease In & Ease Out
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains easing as the distribution of frames around important poses or keyframes to control perceived speed. It distinguishes ease out, ease in, and combined easing, showing how compressed frames create slower movement near a keyframe and larger gaps create faster travel. It is for artists learning to shape the flow and rhythm of animation through timing.
KEY TOPICS:
- Easing as a timing concept
- Ease out
- Ease in
- Ease in and out of a mid-frame
- Timing charts
- Frame compression and speed
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Timing charts
- Compressing drawings around keyframes
- Redistributing a fixed number of frames
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with an evenly spaced sequence as a baseline.
2. For an ease out, compress the early frames toward the first keyframe, leaving fewer frames for the later travel.
3. For an ease in, compress the later frames toward the final keyframe.
4. For ease in and out, compress frames around the relevant mid-frame while the beginning and ending travel remains faster.
5. Combine multiple easings when the action requires several changes of speed, distributing the available frames to create the intended flow.
