TITLE: Lightning Zap - Painting & Commentary
OVERVIEW: This lesson turns a lightning thumbnail into a painted concept with a strong center impact and two distinct beats. It demonstrates lasso-based shape blocking, positive/negative shape design, dimensional ground-plane forms, layered value/color, subtle glow, and iterative feedback-driven refinement. It is aimed at artists practicing graphic but volumetric lightning concepts.
KEY TOPICS:
- Thumbnail-to-concept workflow
- Lightning shape language
- Positive and negative shapes
- Three-dimensional form in flat shapes
- Thick and thin variation
- Layer Styles and glow
- Value hierarchy
- Design iteration and feedback
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Lasso tool for sharp blocking and carving
- Ctrl-click layer thumbnail for alpha selection
- Select > Modify > Contract
- Layer Styles: Stroke, Inner Glow, Outer Glow
- Mask on a color layer
- Soft and crisp brushes
- Linear Dodge/Add
- Layer opacity
- Reference and palette exploration
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Choose the lightning thumbnail and resize/reposition the composition so the tall strike has enough room while respecting the gameplay AOE when required.
2. Block sharp jagged lightning shapes with the Lasso, thinking about the negative spaces between them and how those spaces imply three-dimensional flow.
3. Build the ground and upward bowl/bending shapes so individual forms support the central impact and read as 3D before rendering.
4. Separate dark/red/value layers and use Layer Styles to establish an emissive body, then add graphic internal shapes rather than leaving large areas as flat black.
5. Keep the hottest value focused at the impact and use orange between black and white/yellow so the transition is not just abrupt black-white contrast.
6. Add small sparks and supporting elements, keep glow subtle, and use feedback to split unclear transition timing into clearer impact and dissipation beats.
7. Experiment with alternate color palettes while preserving the underlying value and shape relationships.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Reference other artists and nature rather than trying to invent every shape from nothing.
- Keep the silhouette crisp and intentional.
- Use thick/thin variation and supporting secondary shapes to reinforce motion.
- Keep the highest contrast at the intended impact/focal point.
- Avoid overusing glow; subtle halos are enough to imply emissive light.
- Iterate and throw away weak versions rather than becoming attached to the first idea.
