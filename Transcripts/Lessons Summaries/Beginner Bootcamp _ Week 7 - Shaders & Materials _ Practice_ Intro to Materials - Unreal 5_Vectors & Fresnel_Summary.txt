TITLE: Vectors & Fresnel
OVERVIEW: This lesson introduces the dot product, normalized vectors, camera direction, and Fresnel as practical tools for controlling an effect based on surface orientation. It builds the math visually, then uses the result to create an edge/angle-based mask that can drive opacity, color, and stylized lighting. It is intended for artists who want to understand enough vector math to create useful VFX shaders in Unreal.
KEY TOPICS:
- Dot product
- Vector projection
- Normalize
- Camera direction
- Surface normals
- Fresnel/rim effects
- Angle-based masks
- Exponential shaping
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Dot Product
- Normalize
- Camera Vector
- Normal Vector
- Subtract/Add vector operations
- Power
- Clamp-like value shaping
- Opacity
- Emissive/Color outputs
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Compare two directions with Dot Product and observe that aligned vectors produce a high result while perpendicular/opposite directions reduce it.
2. Normalize vectors when their length should not affect the directional comparison.
3. Use the camera direction and surface normal together to produce a view-dependent value.
4. Feed that value into opacity or color to create a Fresnel-style edge response.
5. Use Power to concentrate the effect toward the desired range and tune the result for the material.
6. Apply the same directional mask to curved meshes and experiment with different powers to create stylized rims and magical effects.
