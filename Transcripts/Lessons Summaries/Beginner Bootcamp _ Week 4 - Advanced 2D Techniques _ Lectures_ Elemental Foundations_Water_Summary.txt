TITLE: Water
OVERVIEW: This lesson provides a beginner foundation for animating water as a family of related liquid behaviors rather than a single fixed effect. It focuses on simple-to-complex water design, cause and effect, splash and air-pocket behavior, dissipation, refraction, and viscosity. It is aimed at beginners who need a practical framework for approaching splashes, waves, rivers, waterfalls, and other liquid effects.
KEY TOPICS:
- Water and liquid design
- Splash construction
- Cause and effect
- Air pockets and secondary jets
- Wire-mesh/3D visualization
- Water dissipation
- Refraction
- Viscosity
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Simple line-and-fill design
- Multiple depth/color layers
- Wire-mesh construction for visualization
- Onion Skin
- Negative-space breakup
- Droplet/bead tracking
- Real-life water/light reference
- Timing-based viscosity studies
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a simple two-color water design before adding multiple depth and foam layers.
2. Identify the energy causing the water to move and build the splash or wave as a response to that force.
3. Use simple 3D guides or wire-mesh lines when the volume is hard to visualize.
4. For a falling object splash, show the air pocket created by the object and the secondary jet caused when water rushes back in.
5. Dissipate airborne sheets by opening negative-space pockets, keeping larger beads tracked frame by frame and allowing tiny pieces to disappear more quickly.
6. Study refraction visually through real water and light, then use timing to distinguish liquids of different viscosity.
7. Practice a simple splash first, then try changing the material to thicker liquids and see whether timing alone can communicate the difference.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Start with a simple water splash.
- Use real-life water reference frequently.
- Track larger droplets carefully during dissipation; small pieces can disappear sooner.
- Do not confuse water with the only kind of liquid; use viscosity and timing to study syrup, oil, milk, lava, or other liquids.
- Keep complexity under control because every additional color/depth layer increases animation workload.
