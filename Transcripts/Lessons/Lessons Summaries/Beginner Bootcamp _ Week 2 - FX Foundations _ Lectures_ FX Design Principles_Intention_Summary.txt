TITLE: Intention
OVERVIEW: This lesson teaches artists to identify why an effect exists in a scene before deciding how to animate it. It uses a “who, what, why, where” approach that considers purpose, placement, scale, interaction, dependencies, and the story context. It is intended for artists who want their FX choices to communicate rather than simply decorate the shot.
KEY TOPICS:
- Purpose of the effect
- Who/what/why questions
- Placement and scale
- Interactions and dependencies
- Story context
- Narrative emphasis
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Scene/storyboard review
- Scale checks
- Composition planning
- Adding secondary details such as smoke or debris when justified
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Ask what the effect is and what it needs to accomplish in the scene.
2. Determine where it belongs and what scale makes sense for the camera and environment.
3. Identify whether it interacts with a character, object, or location and plan those interactions.
4. Check dependencies and continuity across the surrounding shots so scale and behavior remain consistent.
5. Consider the emotional or narrative purpose and add secondary details only when they support that purpose.
6. Reevaluate the effect when the story context changes; the same base design may need adaptation.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Read the storyboard or scene context before locking in an FX approach.
- Keep the effect's narrative purpose in mind while designing it.
- Check scale from the actual camera view.
- Add extra elements only when they support the story or intended focus.
