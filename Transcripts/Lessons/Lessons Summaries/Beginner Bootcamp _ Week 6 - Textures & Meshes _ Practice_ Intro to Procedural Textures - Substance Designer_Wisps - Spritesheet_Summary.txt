TITLE: Wisps - Spritesheet
OVERVIEW: This lesson builds a procedural wisp generator and turns several parameter variations into a 2x2 sprite sheet. It starts from a simple circular/form-based shape, introduces procedural noise, subtraction, mid- and small-scale detail, and deformation, then exposes the result for reuse. It is intended for artists creating versatile magical, fire, or energy wisps procedurally.
KEY TOPICS:
- Wisp sprite-sheet generation
- Procedural shape variation

STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Analyze the wisp as a rounded base with a sharper central flow and establish the main shape.
2. Use Tile Sampler noise to create irregular smaller forms and randomize their positions so the pattern fills the space without repeating uniformly.
3. Subtract the noise from the base and multiply in Perlin-based mid-detail.
4. Apply Non-Uniform Blur/Auto Levels where needed, then use a smaller triangular detail pattern and Multi-Directional Warp to create the final wispy deformation.
5. Remap the finished grayscale with Levels and output the procedural generator.
6. Create multiple parameter variations and place them into a 2x2 sprite sheet for reuse.
7. Test the finished sheet in an energy/fire-like effect and combine it with another fadeout technique in the particle material when appropriate.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Keep the central flow visible while adding procedural detail.
- Expose parameters that help make meaningful variations.
- Avoid repeating the exact same wisp shape when assembling the sprite sheet.
- Test the output in a real effect because a procedural grayscale can read differently once animated and blended.
