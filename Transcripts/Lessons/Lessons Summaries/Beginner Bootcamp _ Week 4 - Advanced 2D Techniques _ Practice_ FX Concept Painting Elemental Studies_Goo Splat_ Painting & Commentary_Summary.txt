TITLE: Goo Splat - Painting & Commentary
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates turning a toxic-goo thumbnail into a complete painted concept while preserving strong shape relationships and a clear area of effect. It covers ground-plane perspective, translucent reflection, thick-and-thin liquid shapes, tangents, layered color, and focal depth. It is intended for artists learning how to render a stylized viscous material with deliberate form and lighting.
KEY TOPICS:
- Thumbnail-to-painting process
- Ground-plane perspective
- Goo surface tension
- Positive/negative shapes
- Tangents
- Reflection and translucency
- Thick and thin shape language
- Value and depth hierarchy
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Brush and Lasso tools
- Layer masks
- Control-click alpha selection
- Inner Glow
- Linear Dodge/Add
- Gradient Overlay set to Color
- Smart Object
- Soft brush with low opacity/flow
- Layer-by-layer color adjustments
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Select a promising goo thumbnail and adjust its position on the template to leave useful space for the upward action.
2. Block the ground-plane shapes first so the later upward strands have a perspective relationship to the surface.
3. Build the main goo silhouette from the outside inward, using subdued background colors and stronger foreground values.
4. Keep negative holes clear, avoid awkward tangents, and use connected shapes/tendrils to imply how the material was pulled apart.
5. Render a droplet by considering reflected light, internal light scatter, and bounce light from the ground.
6. Use round tips for detached liquid because liquid retracts into rounded forms; combine thick and thin shapes according to implied speed.
7. Preserve strong outer silhouettes while adding soft color, glow, and subtle interior variation on separate layers.
8. Use smart-object/gradient-overlay color experiments only as controlled variations; preserve deliberate layer-by-layer color relationships for the final treatment.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Do not be afraid to throw away a few minutes of work when a shape is not working.
- Keep silhouettes crisp during foundational passes; softness can be added later.
- Think about positive and negative shapes together.
- Avoid awkwardly kissing/tangent lines between overlapping forms.
- Use reference for reflections, translucency, and surface behavior.
- Move through the whole painting instead of polishing only one local area.
- Keep major shape layers separate when later opacity/color control will be useful.
