TITLE: Missile Wind-up & Impact
OVERVIEW: This lesson blocks out a full missile sequence using a wind-up, missile core and trail, and an existing impact effect. It uses kitbashing to work efficiently, then applies squash and stretch, overlapping action, follow-through, trails, and deliberately shortened physical behavior so the gameplay read stays clear. It is aimed at beginners learning how animation principles translate into multi-emitter Niagara effects.
KEY TOPICS:
- Kitbashing
- Wind-up and anticipation
- Overlapping action
- Follow-through
- Squash and stretch
- Ribbon trails
- Velocity alignment
- Trail cleanup
- Gameplay readability
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Ctrl+D to duplicate
- Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V to copy emitters
- Add Comment around emitters
- Niagara template/radio-burst system
- Ribbon Trail Leader / Follower
- Velocity Align
- Linear Velocity
- Non-uniform Scale Sprite Size
- Location Event
- Spawn Per Unit
- Emitter Age curve
- System loop duration
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Duplicate the existing hit-impact emitters and disable them as a reusable impact group rather than rebuilding the impact from scratch.
2. Create a new Niagara system from a radio-burst/template source and copy its Ribbon Trail Leader and Follower emitters into the missile system.
3. Configure the missile core as a single velocity-aligned triangle particle with short lifetime and non-uniform size.
4. Use a non-uniform Scale Sprite Size curve to squash and stretch the missile before launch, during rapid travel, and at the collision.
5. Build the ribbon trail and decide whether normal Spawn Rate or Spawn Per Unit better matches the missile's variable movement through world space.
6. Re-enable and modify the impact so sparks/trails provide follow-through after the missile stops, but deliberately shorten or simplify anything that distracts from the gameplay event.
7. Treat overlapping action as the trailing motion caused by the primary action and follow-through as the remaining momentum after the impact.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Reuse existing emitters and modify them rather than rebuilding common pieces.
- Keep physicality in service of readability; the instructor intentionally shortens true follow-through when it becomes distracting.
- Use Spawn Per Unit for variable-speed world-space motion when appropriate.
- Think of trail motion as a connected secondary action rather than an isolated decoration.
- Keep complex multi-stage effects separable enough that gameplay systems can spawn and control their parts independently.
