TITLE: Sparker & Aquanna Illustration - Painting & Commentary
OVERVIEW: This lesson is a full sped-up walkthrough of a final fire-and-water character illustration, from thumbnails through composition, value, color, and detailed painting. It demonstrates tackling one problem at a time, using references for physical behavior and color, maintaining character focus, and revising or removing elements when they compete with the image. It is intended as a holistic painting-process study rather than a single technique lesson.
KEY TOPICS:
- Thumbnail exploration
- Composition and focal point
- Flats and shape blocking
- Value hierarchy
- Color development
- Water rendering
- Fire rendering
- Reference use
- Iteration and revision
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Black-and-white thumbnails
- Photoshop flats/masks
- Layered value and color passes
- Reference images in the PSD
- Soft and hard brushes
- Layer-separated foreground/background elements
- Value hierarchy and saturation control
- Smudging/softening used selectively
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Create several small thumbnails to explore the relationship between the two characters and identify the intended focal point.
2. Choose the strongest composition, then block flat shapes so later colors can be clipped or painted cleanly.
3. Work sequentially through sketch, shape design, value, and color rather than solving every dimension at once.
4. Keep the characters as the main focal point and use background water/fire layers to create depth without stealing attention.
5. Use physical references for fire, water, lighting, and color; translate observed behavior into the stylized forms rather than copying the reference literally.
6. Revise aggressively: remove foreground waves or redo seafoam when they compete with the character, and let later passes replace weaker earlier decisions.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Explore several thumbnail directions before committing to a major illustration.
- Keep thumbnails small and do not spend too long perfecting one.
- Solve composition, shape design, value, and color in stages.
- Use references constantly for difficult materials such as fire and water.
- Do not become attached to any individual pass; remove or replace elements when the image benefits.
- Keep focal characters readable through value, saturation, and surrounding rest space.
