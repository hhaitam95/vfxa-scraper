TITLE: Stretch & Smear
OVERVIEW: This lesson introduces stretching and smear frames as ways to bridge extremely fast actions that the eye cannot fully resolve between start and finish. It presents several stylistic approaches, including large smears, ghosting, speedlines, and overshoot, while emphasizing that the exact look depends on the intended style. It is aimed at artists who want another tool for enhancing high-speed animation.
KEY TOPICS:
- Stretch frames
- Smear frames
- Ghosting
- Speedlines
- Overshoot
- Anticipation combined with smears
- Style-driven design
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Frame-by-frame smear design
- Dry-brush look as a historical smear technique
- Ghosting/tracer shapes
- Speedlines to imply volume
- Overshoot and correction
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Identify an action that is fast enough that the eye may miss the transition.
2. Decide which smear language fits the style: enlarged single drawing, skewed/elongated form, ghosted remnants, or speedlines.
3. Place the smear between the start and end poses, using anticipation when appropriate.
4. Preserve some visual language from the preceding form so the smear connects the motion rather than becoming an unrelated drawing.
5. Resolve the smear into the final pose, optionally using an overshoot before settling.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Try several smear styles rather than assuming there is one correct construction.
- Let the art direction or style guide the final look of the smear.
- Use smears where they clarify fast motion; do not add them without a reason.
