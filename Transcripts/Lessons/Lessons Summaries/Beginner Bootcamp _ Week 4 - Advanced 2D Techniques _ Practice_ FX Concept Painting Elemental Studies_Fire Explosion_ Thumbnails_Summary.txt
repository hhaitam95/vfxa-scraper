TITLE: Fire Explosion - Thumbnails
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates how to generate several black-and-white thumbnail directions for a fiery explosion before committing to a full painting. It explores radial shape language, smoke and fire variations, negative space, asymmetry, and center-directed motion while keeping the drawings quick. It is aimed at artists learning to present multiple visual directions efficiently.
KEY TOPICS:
- Black-and-white thumbnails
- Client/director options
- Radial AOE design
- Shape language
- Negative space
- Asymmetry
- Thick and thin
- Reference gathering
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- F key for the recorded screen/interface mode
- Photoshop layer labeling
- Horizontal canvas flip for checking asymmetry
- Quick black-and-white blocking
- Sketchbook/reference collection
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start from the requested effect and create several quick black-and-white options rather than refining a single idea.
2. Explore different radial constructions: splash-like forms, cylindrical sheets, bubble-like shapes, and spiky outward shapes.
3. Keep the motion directed away from the center and vary the silhouette while preserving a simple larger organizing shape.
4. Check the thumbnail at a glance and flip the canvas to expose asymmetry or uneven distribution.
5. Use real-world slow-motion references such as paint-filled water balloons or other explosions to study force and breakup.
6. Select the strongest direction for the full concept and carry the thumbnail's general intent forward.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Make several quick thumbnails rather than polishing one.
- Stay loose; thumbnails are for direction, not final detail.
- Keep complexity organized inside a larger simple shape.
- Keep a sketchbook and collect useful references for future ideas.
- Use real reference to understand how liquids and explosions break apart, not only other artists' drawings.
