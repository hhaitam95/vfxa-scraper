TITLE: Carving Out Shapes
OVERVIEW: This lesson teaches another disappearance method: carving negative space out of a shape while preserving the material identity of the thing being animated. A goopy liquid dissolve is built with directional carving guides, varied timing, tension, bridges, and droplets. It is aimed at artists who want to turn a simple dissolve into a more believable material-specific animation.
KEY TOPICS:
- Negative-space carving
- Dissolve motion guides
- Material-specific shape language
- Surface tension and sticky bridges
- Droplets and secondary motion
- Staggered carving timing
- Working one branch at a time
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Guide layers
- Onion Skin
- Copy and paste frames
- Eraser for carving
- Frame-by-frame review
- Zooming for corner cleanup
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Design the final shape and create guide lines that establish the main directions of the dissolve.
2. Hold the shape briefly so the viewer can read it, then copy frames and begin carving small areas along the guide.
3. Increase and decrease carving speed selectively, allowing different carving branches to gain or lose energy at different times.
4. Modify the carved edges to reflect the material, such as goopy edges, stretched bridges, tension, and droplets instead of generic holes.
5. Work on one carving branch at a time and use the remaining branches to create changing focal points.
6. In the final pass, inspect spacing and corners, add subtle movement to leftover pieces, and let the final droplets shrink away.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Plan carving directions before drawing the final animation.
- Do not treat the object as generic blobs; preserve the shape language of the material.
- Avoid identical carving timing across every branch.
- Review the motion repeatedly and fix spacing or corner behavior that reads stiff.
- The same idea can be adapted to moving elements as long as the carving supports the main motion.
