TITLE: Lightning & Flash Frames
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains how lightning differs from slower elemental effects and how its near-instant appearance should influence timing. It covers lightning creation and dissipation, flash-frame/color-card techniques, drop frames, environmental interaction, and sharp shape changes. It is aimed at beginners learning to make lightning read quickly and energetically.
KEY TOPICS:
- Lightning timing
- Lightning creation and dissipation
- Flash frames
- Color cards
- Detailed flash frames
- Drop/blank frames
- Shape snapping and holds
- Lightning reference
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Color card/full-screen color frame
- Blank/drop frames
- Black/white environment inversions
- Quick shape holds followed by abrupt changes
- Real-life lightning reference
- Spark/twinkle leftovers on dissipation
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Treat lightning as essentially appearing or disappearing rather than visibly traveling across the screen unless travel is specifically required.
2. Create lightning by growing the design/thickness into view over a very short span, then dissipate it in reverse or with small residual sparks.
3. Add simple color-card flash frames for impact, optionally matching the lightning's color.
4. For important shots, use more detailed flash frames that interact with the background; for less important lightning, keep the flash simpler.
5. Mix in blank/drop frames to create stuttered, erratic rhythm, then balance them with the lightning's own holds and snaps.
6. Hold a general lightning shape for a few frames before abruptly switching to a new shape so the eye registers a design before it changes.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Practice lightning strikes and combine them with drop frames and flash frames.
- Use real-life lightning footage to study timing and shape.
- Do not automatically animate travel; decide whether the effect needs to move across screen.
- Match flash-frame complexity to the importance of the shot.
- Experiment until the visual rhythm feels right; the lesson presents the techniques as approaches rather than rigid rules.
