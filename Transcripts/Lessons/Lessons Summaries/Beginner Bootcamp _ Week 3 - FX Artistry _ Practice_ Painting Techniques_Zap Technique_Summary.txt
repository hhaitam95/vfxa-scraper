TITLE: Zap Technique
OVERVIEW: This lesson demonstrates how to paint a hard, jagged electrical effect inside a rounded guide shape. The instructor blocks the energetic red form with a hard brush, uses selection contraction and dark internal layers for depth, then adds white/yellow glow accents and sharp edge cleanup. It is aimed at artists learning to render a graphic lightning material with strong shape language and controlled glow.
KEY TOPICS:
- Hard-edged lightning shape design
- Red and black value structure
- Selection contraction
- Cavities and directional motion
- Edge cleanup
- Inner/Outer Glow
- Canvas rotation for easier line control
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Hard pressure-sensitive brush
- Lasso selection
- Select > Modify > Contract
- Ctrl-H to hide selection
- Hard eraser
- Canvas Rotate tool
- Outer Glow
- Inner Glow
- Normal blending
- Linear Dodge/Add as used in the course workflow
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a hard, size-responsive brush because the zap uses harsh, jagged, spiky shapes.
2. Block the main red electrical form and refine it with the Lasso rather than trying to make every contour perfect in one brush pass.
3. Contract the selection and place darker internal material on separate layers, using the form's contours to reinforce the intended motion.
4. Clean the outer edge with a hard eraser and shape the silhouette deliberately rather than accepting accidental flat or square areas.
5. Rotate the canvas when a desired stroke direction fights the natural hand/wrist angle.
6. Add white/yellow hot accents and adjust Outer/Inner Glow settings to create the final luminous punch.
7. Leave room for small residual red bits or secondary accents only when they help the form.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Use hard edges for graphic lightning rather than starting with a soft brush.
- Refine large shapes with brush plus lasso instead of over-designing every contour.
- Let discovery happen during the cleanup pass; the instructor encourages improving the original idea when a better shape appears.
- Rotate the canvas when the natural hand angle makes a stroke difficult.
- Keep sharp edges and strong silhouette changes as core parts of the zap's visual identity.
