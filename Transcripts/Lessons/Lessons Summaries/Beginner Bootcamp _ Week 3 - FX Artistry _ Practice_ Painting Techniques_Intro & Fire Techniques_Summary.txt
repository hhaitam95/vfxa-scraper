TITLE: Intro & Fire Techniques
OVERVIEW: This lesson introduces the painting approach used for the course's hand-painted effect concepts, then demonstrates rendering a fiery translucent material from a simple painted base. The instructor combines a small set of Photoshop brushes, selections, layer styles, adjustment ideas, and iterative painting to construct smoke, internal fire, glow, and sparks. It is aimed at students learning a practical hand-painted FX workflow rather than a large collection of complex brushes.
KEY TOPICS:
- Hand-painted FX workflow
- Brush and chisel-brush setup
- Pen tilt and brush spacing
- Smoke rendering
- Fire and internal luminance
- Layer organization
- Linear Dodge/Additive blending
- Multiply for darkening
- Non-destructive selection/masking
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Default Photoshop round brush at different softness levels
- Chisel brush using Shape Dynamics > pen tilt
- Brush spacing adjustment
- Pen-pressure opacity
- History panel
- Ctrl-click layer for marquee selection
- Ctrl-H to hide selection edges
- Alt for temporary Eyedropper
- Brightness/Contrast and Hue/Saturation adjustments
- Linear Dodge/Add
- Multiply
- Soft eraser
- Lasso tool with Alt/Shift selection modifiers
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a simple default round brush or a chisel brush configured with pen tilt, and correct spacing so the brush stroke is smooth.
2. Use a low-opacity copy of the underlying ball/reference as a guide and Ctrl-click its layer to constrain new painting to the correct area.
3. Establish the material palette and lighting logic, then paint the dark translucent smoke base using soft shapes.
4. Carve and soften smoke shapes while adding secondary blobs, then place distinct fire shapes in the darker interior regions.
5. Build the fire from darks toward oranges/yellows/whites, preserving a consistent gradient so bright internal colors do not touch black abruptly.
6. Add glow layers using Linear Dodge/Add and keep the layer names organized so adjustments land on the intended layer.
7. Add subtle darks with Multiply and use history, selections, and masks to correct mistakes without destroying useful painting.
8. Finish with sparks, optional glow, and a careful review of the whole material treatment.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- You do not need many fancy brushes; the instructor uses a small, repeatable set.
- Keep layers clearly named, especially in larger working files.
- Use the selection to constrain broad painting and avoid accidental spill.
- Preserve intermediate color steps between dark and bright regions.
- Use the Eraser and Brush together to shape forms rather than forcing every stroke to be perfect.
- Use real flame slow-motion reference later to understand the form language more deeply.
