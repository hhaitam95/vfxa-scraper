TITLE: Sketch - Drawing from a Reference
OVERVIEW: This lesson applies the preceding construction and line-weight ideas to a fire-on-a-flower reference. The instructor first builds the flower from simple guides and then places a large fire shape over it, adding secondary flame forms, overlaps, and line-weight changes to keep the drawing readable. It is aimed at students practicing the complete reference-to-stylized-sketch workflow.
KEY TOPICS:
- Reference breakdown
- Simple-form construction
- Petal layout and symmetry
- Fire shape construction
- Overlap
- Line weight
- Visual hierarchy
- Implied transparency
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Red construction guides followed by black final lines
- Cones and ovals for the flower base
- Dividing six petals into three large and three slimmer petals
- Overlap lines
- Line-weight emphasis
- Thin lines to imply lighter or more transparent material
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Establish the flower base with a cone/oval construction and divide the six petals into large and slim groups.
2. Build the petal folds, overlaps, and inner center before committing to final contour lines.
3. Block the main fire mass as one large simple shape, then add smaller internal flames and connecting pieces.
4. Simplify crowded regions instead of adding detail everywhere, especially where many petals already converge.
5. Redraw the important contours in darker black lines and use line weight to distinguish foreground, overlap, and focal areas.
6. Use thinner lines in selected flame areas to suggest transparency or reduced visual prominence.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Keep the first construction simple and establish the big shapes before details.
- Simplify crowded regions rather than adding every possible element.
- Use overlaps to clarify which forms are in front.
- Use line weight deliberately to establish hierarchy and readability.
