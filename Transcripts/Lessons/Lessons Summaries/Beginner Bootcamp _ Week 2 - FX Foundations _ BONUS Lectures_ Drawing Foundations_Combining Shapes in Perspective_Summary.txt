TITLE: Combining Shapes in Perspective
OVERVIEW: This lesson shows how to construct complex forms by starting with simple shapes and extending, intersecting, subtracting, and rounding them in perspective. It uses cubes, pyramids, cylinders, and organic forms to demonstrate how intersection points and perspective-aligned edges keep complex drawings coherent. It is for artists who want to move beyond isolated primitives.
KEY TOPICS:
- Building complex forms from primitives
- Perspective-aligned intersections
- Cubes, pyramids, cylinders, and cones
- Subtracting shapes
- Rounded and organic forms
- Visible edge definition
- Shading for volume
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Perspective plane/grid
- Diagonal lines for finding square centers
- Vertical construction lines
- Perspective-aligned construction lines
- Light construction strokes followed by heavier final lines
- Simple shading to clarify volume
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Establish a perspective plane and draw a simple base form such as a cube.
2. Add or subtract another primitive by finding its intersection points with the existing form.
3. Extend those intersection points using the perspective directions and verticals to reconstruct the new shape.
4. Define only the visible edges more strongly and use lighter construction lines where appropriate.
5. Add rounding by defining where a corner begins and ends, then replacing the sharp edge with an arc that follows the form.
6. Add interior curves and shading to make the combined form read as three-dimensional.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Start complex drawings from simple primitives.
- Keep intersection points and perspective directions consistent.
- Use lighter construction lines before committing to visible edges.
- Add shading only after the structural drawing works.
