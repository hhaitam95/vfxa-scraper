TITLE: Small to Big
OVERVIEW: This lesson teaches how to make shapes appear by growing from a small starting state into their final size while varying how the growth responds to force. Two blobs are animated with different expansion directions and timing so they do not feel identical. It is aimed at artists practicing controlled, readable appearances with simple shapes.
KEY TOPICS:
- Small-to-big appearance
- Directional expansion
- Force-based asymmetry
- Timing differences between elements
- Moving holds
- Shape variety
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Onion Skin
- Brush with fixed size/no flow
- Frame-by-frame redraw
- Eraser for cleanup
- Frame extension
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Decide where the force is acting on the shape and choose which side should expand more.
2. Start with a small blob and expand it toward the final form, decreasing the amount of change as it settles.
3. Add directional asymmetry so the growth does not happen evenly around the center.
4. Animate a second shape with different start timing, expansion amount, or force direction.
5. Use subtle repeated frames as a moving hold instead of stopping abruptly, then review for wobble and clean up as needed.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Vary the side and amount of expansion based on the imagined force.
- Do not make every object settle on the same frame.
- Use moving holds for subtle deceleration.
- Try the same principles with lines, ovals, squares, or triangles.
