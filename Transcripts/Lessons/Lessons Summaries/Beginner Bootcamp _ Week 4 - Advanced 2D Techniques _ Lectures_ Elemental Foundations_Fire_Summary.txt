TITLE: Fire
OVERVIEW: This lesson builds a practical foundation for understanding and animating fire from small candle flames to larger fires and infernos. It combines simple shape design with a 3D mental model, fuel/air behavior, speed, intensity, and dissipation. It is intended for beginners who want a repeatable way to reason about fire instead of relying only on intuition.
KEY TOPICS:
- Candle-fire construction
- 2D drawing with a 3D mental model
- Fuel and oxygen
- Fire intensity and speed
- Pyramid/triangle fire structure
- Negative-space dissipation
- Design consistency
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Triangle/pyramid construction guide
- Negative-space guides
- Timing charts
- Key poses and in-betweens
- Frame-count planning
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a simple candle flame and keep the design appropriate to the small scale instead of overcomplicating the interior.
2. Think of the fire as a three-dimensional volume even though it is drawn on a 2D plane; the hottest area is near the fuel source.
3. Use a triangle/pyramid as a mental or visual guide, with fire strongest at the base and pinching as energy decreases toward the top.
4. Animate the shape with attention to air movement, rising heat, changing direction, and layered foreground/background licks.
5. Use speed to communicate intensity: faster creation and dissipation reads more energetic, while slower motion can read calmer.
6. Keep the visual language consistent, choosing angular or rounded shapes according to the intended style.
7. For dissipation, open negative space until the fire breaks into smaller pieces and finally disappears.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Start with candle fire before attempting larger infernos.
- Keep a consistent shape theme throughout the animation.
- Use timing intentionally to change the perceived intensity.
- Practice the fire burst repeatedly; the instructor suggests doing it four or five times across a session or a few days.
- Scrap and restart quickly when an attempt is not working rather than spending too long repairing it.
