TITLE: The Wave Principle
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains the wave principle as a reusable way to animate flexible or wavy forms, from a simple line to a three-dimensional folded shape. It gradually increases difficulty from an anchored line to a volumetric wave and finally to a moving anchor point. It is aimed at beginners who want to understand a core motion pattern that transfers to many effects and props.
KEY TOPICS:
- Wave principle
- Anchored waves
- Free-floating ends
- 2D line waves
- 3D/folded waves
- Moving anchors
- Timing charts
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Wave/arc guides
- Anchor-point planning
- Timing charts
- Onion Skin
- Simple line-to-volume progression
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Start with a single anchored line and animate the free end using the wave principle.
2. Keep the anchor fixed while the flexible portion swings or folds through its arcs.
3. Increase difficulty by giving the line more dimension and tracking folds as parts move toward and away from the viewer.
4. Add internal pieces or packed forms only after the main wave remains coherent through the timing.
5. For the advanced version, animate the anchor itself moving across the line and allow the wave to react to the new timing.
6. Practice each level in order, moving from the simple line to dimension and finally the moving-base version.

- Use the principle for other effects and objects that behave like flexible or wavy forms.
