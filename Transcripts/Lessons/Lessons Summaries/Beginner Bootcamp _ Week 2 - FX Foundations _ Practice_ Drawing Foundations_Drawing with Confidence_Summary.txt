TITLE: Drawing with Confidence
OVERVIEW: This lesson presents a daily drawing warmup built around confident straight lines, curves, circles, and controlled line pressure. It emphasizes using the elbow, shoulder, and whole arm instead of relying only on the wrist, aiming before each stroke, and accepting mistakes instead of erasing them. It is intended for beginners who want more accurate and confident hand movements for sketching and cleanup.
KEY TOPICS:
- Confident straight strokes
- Curved strokes
- Circles and ovals
- Drawing from the elbow and shoulder
- Stroke planning
- Line pressure
- Slow versus fast drawing
- Daily practice
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- A4 paper and ballpoint pen
- Aiming in the air before landing a stroke
- Connecting dots with fast strokes
- Grid-based circle practice
- Marker for stronger visible pressure variation
- Drawing from elbow or shoulder
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Place dots across the page and connect them with straight and curved strokes, changing the spacing so both short and long gestures are practiced.
2. Aim the stroke in the air first, then make a light gesture and commit to the stronger stroke without stopping at the target.
3. Practice straight lines slowly in multiple directions, using the whole arm and focusing on even spacing and controlled pressure.
4. Practice curves and waves in multiple directions, learning to finish each gesture smoothly so one curve can connect naturally into another.
5. Build a grid and practice circles of consistent size and smoothness, correcting angular starts or ends with a new stroke rather than erasing.
6. Practice changing pressure from thick to thin in straight and curved strokes, then rotate the exercises across different days rather than doing the entire routine in one sitting.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Use a ballpoint pen rather than a pencil so mistakes cannot simply be erased.
- Aim before each stroke and commit to the movement.
- Do not be afraid to move beyond the target dot.
- Use the elbow and shoulder for larger strokes instead of only the wrist.
- Practice all directions over time, but stop or vary the routine if the hand becomes painful.
- Make pressure transitions smooth rather than abruptly changing thickness.
