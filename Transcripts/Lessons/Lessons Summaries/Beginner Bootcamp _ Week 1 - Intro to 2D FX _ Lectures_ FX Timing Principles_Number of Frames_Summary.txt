TITLE: Number of Frames
OVERVIEW: This lesson gives a broad introduction to timing as everything that happens between a starting and ending frame. It explains how frame placement, mid-frame position, timing charts, and animating on ones, twos, or threes change the feel and workload of an animation. It is intended as a foundational timing overview for beginners.
KEY TOPICS:
- Timing between start and end poses
- Mid-frame placement
- Timing charts
- Frames per second
- Animating on ones, twos, threes, and fours
- Balancing smoothness and workload
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Timing charts for shorthand planning
- Frame-by-frame placement
- Animating on ones/twos/threes
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Establish the starting and final frames and decide the broad path from point A to point B.
2. Choose the mid-frame and recognize that its placement changes the perceived speed of different parts of the action.
3. Use a timing chart to describe frame spacing without drawing every frame immediately.
4. Decide whether the action should use every frame or be held on twos, threes, or another exposure pattern.
5. Judge the final visual result and adjust timing/frame density based on what still looks good.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Use timing charts as notes for yourself or for other artists.
- Remember that not every frame in a second must contain a unique drawing.
- Increase drawing density when a lower exposure count no longer looks good.
- Judge timing by the visible result rather than by technique labels alone.
