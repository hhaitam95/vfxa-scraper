TITLE: Thinking in 3D
OVERVIEW: This lesson presents simple drawing decisions that make flat artwork feel three-dimensional. It focuses on viewer angle, visible thickness, curved interior lines, size changes with distance, and overlapping shapes. It is intended as a practical reminder for artists who need stronger 3D form without fully modeling objects.
KEY TOPICS:
- Camera/view angle
- Shape thickness
- Interior contour lines
- Near/far scale
- Overlapping objects
- Perspective surfaces
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Squashing shapes to suggest view angle
- Adding visible edge thickness
- Curved interior lines to describe volume
- Size scaling with perspective
- Overlapping shapes for depth
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Decide the angle from which the viewer sees the form and deform the silhouette to match it.
2. Add visible thickness where the object is not a flat plane.
3. Use curved interior lines on rounded forms to describe their volume and direction.
4. Make farther objects or regions smaller and nearer ones larger according to perspective.
5. Overlap objects deliberately so the viewer can infer which forms are in front and behind.
6. Apply the same ideas to surfaces such as roads and to irregular organic forms.
