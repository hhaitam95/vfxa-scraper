TITLE: Impulse & Rhythm
OVERVIEW: This lesson distinguishes short, self-contained effects impulses from persistent effects that need a coherent rhythm. It connects animation timing to beats in music and character animation, then uses fire as an example of a cycling effect whose internal actions should share a consistent tempo. It is for artists learning to build timing that feels intentional rather than mechanically uneven.
KEY TOPICS:
- Impulse effects
- Persistent effects and rhythm
- Beats and musical timing
- Cycles
- Dissipation timing
- Changes of tempo
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Timing the effect to audio or character beats
- Path-of-action planning for cycles
- Frame-count planning for short impulses
- Gradual tempo changes
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Decide whether the effect is an impulse that happens and resolves, or a persistent/cycling effect.
2. For an impulse, establish the burst, keep its main motion moving forward, and dissipate it within the available duration.
3. For a persistent effect, establish the path of action and organize repeated movements so the end can flow back into the beginning.
4. Match small dissipation events to the rhythm of the larger effect instead of letting one element linger much longer without reason.
5. When changing tempo, transition gradually so the new rhythm feels connected to the established one.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Consider the beat of character motion or audio when timing an effect.
- Keep small motions consistent with the rhythm of the overall effect.
- Change tempo gradually when a new timing is needed.
