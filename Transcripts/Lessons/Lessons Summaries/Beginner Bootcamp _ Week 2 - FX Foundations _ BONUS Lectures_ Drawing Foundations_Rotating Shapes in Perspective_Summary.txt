TITLE: Rotating Shapes in Perspective
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains how to draw a shape rotating within an existing perspective scene. It demonstrates how rotation changes a shape's relationship to its vanishing points and uses a projected circle/oval guide to track corner positions. It also presents a faster mental-construction approach for rotating simple forms.
KEY TOPICS:
- Rotating cubes in perspective
- Vanishing-point changes during rotation
- Projection guides
- Rotating around a center point
- Off-center rotation
- Simplified mental construction
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Perspective projection guides
- Circle/oval guides for corner paths
- Perpendicular construction lines
- Light construction strokes
- Onion Skin for comparing animation frames
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Keep the scene's horizon line fixed while recognizing that the rotated object's vanishing directions change.
2. Construct an overhead circle/oval guide representing the path of the object's corners.
3. Project the corner positions from that guide back into the perspective scene.
4. Connect the projected points while checking that the edges converge consistently.
5. For a centered square/cube, simplify the construction by imagining the corners around one circle; for off-center rotations, construct separate circular paths for the corners.
6. In animation, compare the rotated states with Onion Skin and keep construction strokes light until the final edges make sense.

- Use light construction strokes so corrections are easy.
- For rotations around a non-central pivot, give individual corners their own projected paths.
