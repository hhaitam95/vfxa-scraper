TITLE: Physical Properties
OVERVIEW: This lesson explains why effects should be grounded in observed physical behavior even when the final style is highly stylized. It emphasizes studying real-life video and still frames, broadening detailed observations into manageable shapes, and building a visual mental library through repeated reference work. It is intended for artists who want stronger fire, water, smoke, lava, and other material animation.
KEY TOPICS:
- Real-life reference
- Physical behavior of materials
- Shape language from observation
- Simplification
- Visual mental library
- Repeated studies
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Real-life still-frame reference
- Real-life video reference
- Frame-by-frame study
- Broad-shape simplification
- Reference to other artists used cautiously
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Find real visual and video references for the physical material or effect you need to animate.
2. Observe the overall behavior, forces, and large shapes before focusing on individual details.
3. Translate the observed material into broad, animatable shapes instead of copying every small contour.
4. Repeat still-frame and shape studies so the characteristic behavior becomes familiar.
5. Build a mental library of how different materials move, allowing faster decisions when entering new scenes.
