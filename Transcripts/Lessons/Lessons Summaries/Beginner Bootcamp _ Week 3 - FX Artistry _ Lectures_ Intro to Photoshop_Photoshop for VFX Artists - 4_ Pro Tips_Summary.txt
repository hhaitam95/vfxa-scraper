TITLE: Photoshop for VFX Artists - 4: Pro Tips
OVERVIEW: This final Photoshop introduction is a catch-all of practical techniques that make hand-painted VFX work faster and easier to inspect. It covers history-based undo, proof colors for checking values, alpha-based layer selection, screen-space cleanup, Liquify, foreground/background color swapping, and palette sampling. It is intended as a final utility lesson after the earlier Photoshop tools, layers, and adjustment lessons.
KEY TOPICS:
- History and undo/redo
- Proof Colors and value checking
- Alpha-based selections
- Selection refinement
- Liquify
- Foreground/background colors
- Palette sampling
- Screen-space efficiency
TOOLS / TECHNIQUES / SHORTCUTS:
- Ctrl-Z / Ctrl-Shift-Z for history navigation as demonstrated
- Ctrl-Y for Proof Colors in the recorded Photoshop version
- Ctrl-click layer thumbnail to select by alpha
- Select > Modify > Contract/Expand
- Ctrl-H to hide/show selection
- Ctrl-D to deselect
- F to cycle screen-space display modes
- Liquify
- Show Backdrop in Liquify
- X to swap foreground/background colors
STEP-BY-STEP TAKEAWAYS:
1. Use the History panel and the recorded undo/redo shortcuts to move through recent painting changes.
2. Toggle Proof Colors with Ctrl-Y to inspect value structure, focal-point readability, and contrast independently of the visible color treatment.
3. Ctrl-click a layer thumbnail to select its opaque alpha range, then contract or expand that selection and paint on a new layer while preserving the selected transparency.
4. Use Liquify on the correct layer to push and pull shapes while retaining cleaner edge fidelity, checking the result against the backdrop when necessary.
5. Use X to switch between foreground and background colors and keep a small palette nearby for consistent color sampling.

ASSIGNMENTS / TIPS:
- Check values regularly rather than judging an effect only by its color.
- Make alpha selections from the layer thumbnail when you need to preserve existing transparency.
- Use Liquify selectively; the instructor presents it as a useful but not universal tool.
- Keep foreground/background colors and a small palette available for consistent painting.
